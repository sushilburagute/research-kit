# Research handoff contract

Create `research-brief.md` and `evidence-ledger.md` in the article's authorized output directory, or return equivalent sections in chat if file writing is unavailable. Use the sibling `assets/` templates linked directly from SKILL.md. Remove example instructions and fill fields honestly. `unknown`, `not applicable`, and `not checked` are valid disclosures when explained.

## Research brief

Include:

1. Topic, reader, reader problem, tentative angle, as-of date, scope, assumptions, and research questions.
2. Key findings with claim and source IDs. Label interpretations and hypotheses explicitly.
3. Strong counterevidence and disagreements, plus their implications for the tentative angle.
4. Concrete material: actual incidents, worked mechanisms, measured results, or clearly hypothetical examples.
5. Possible story angles with evidence, weaknesses, and author input needed. Do not silently choose the final narrative.
6. Questions only the author can answer and remaining research gaps.
7. A completion report with the chosen outcome, access and capability limits, and work not performed.

Use one completion outcome:

- `ready-for-narrative`: the author has useful material; essential uncertainty is clearly disclosed. This is not a publication or accuracy certification.
- `limited`: access, tools, budget, or time materially restricts coverage.
- `needs-essential-evidence`: an unresolved claim or question is central enough that the planned argument cannot yet be supported.

## Evidence ledger

Keep two registries and a compact research/change log. IDs are stable within the article.

For each source, include ID, title, author/publisher, source type, URL or authorized local locator, publication/update dates, access date, original/upstream source, relevant version or study conditions, and access limitations. Use `unknown` for unavailable dates, not the access date as a substitute publication date.

For each important claim, include ID, current wording, entry type, source IDs, supporting excerpt or precise locator, interpretation of support, verification status, limitations or conflicting evidence, and proposed correction or next step. Mark claims essential to the argument. Treat a short external excerpt as quotation and preserve its context; a model-written summary is not a quotation.

Use the exact entry types and statuses defined in `research-quality.md`. Give non-factual entries `not-applicable`, explain their basis, and do not imply they were independently verified. Essential unconfirmed, disputed, or contradicted claims must appear in the brief's gaps or disagreements.

## Search and change log

Record material questions, query themes, what was actually read, independence issues, failed access attempts, counterevidence searched, and why investigation stopped. Do not dump every tool trace. Record additions, corrections, and superseded claims without rewriting the author's notes or erasing prior evidence.

## Handoff checks

- Confirm each factual finding in the brief resolves to an existing claim and readable source, or carries an explicit evidence gap.
- Distinguish citations from mere related reading. Do not attach a broad source to a more specific unsupported sentence.
- Check factual wording after narrative edits; cite the actual supported version of the claim.
- List missing capabilities, source access failures, and unresolved contradictions plainly.
- Keep article notes, evidence, and draft logically separate even if they share one directory.
- Do not publish, push, send, or rewrite the author's full draft unless the current task authorizes it.
